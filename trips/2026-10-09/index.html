October 9, 2026 · Central Nevada

SR 839 to Finger Rock Road

We left US 50 between Fallon and Middlegate heading south on SR 839. About 15 miles in, we took the Old Cabin Squeeze (Bonny Blue Trail 3) bump-out: roughly 4 miles of narrow two-track east of the highway, tight enough in places that we needed a ground guide, before it dropped us back on SR 839. It was our first time out in a full-width vehicle, so we learned that limit on the fly.

From there it was another 1.2 miles south to where the pavement ended, and so too did the official SR 839. Keeping slightly left (southeast) to stay on Ryan Canyon Road for 9.4 miles brought us to what some maps show as the Deadhorse Wells town site.

We again stayed left (southeast) onto Rabbit Springs Road, 26 miles to SR 361, then about 7 miles north on 361 to the start of Gabbs Range Traverse (Bonny Blue Trail 4).

From SR 361, the Traverse cuts east through the Gabbs Valley Range for 12 miles to Finger Rock Road. About 7 miles in, Washout Canyon Spur (Bonny Blue Trail 4 Spur) climbs, then drops into a canyon over 3.3 miles south. We'd planned to exit through that canyon, which we got through a few years ago in a smaller vehicle. It has since washed out, and as of October 2026 full-size 4x4s can't get through, so we turned around and went back to the Traverse.

Where the Traverse ends at Finger Rock Road, you have options. North takes you back toward SR 361 and Gabbs. South, a few miles on, Finger Rock Road meets Dump Run Road. Turn west there to stay on maintained dirt to US 95 near Mina. That's how we got out, 18 miles from the end of the trail. Turn east instead for Gabbs Pole Line Road, the longest way out.

We turned off US 50 onto SR 839 right where the 1861 Trail passes. It's one of the Nevada Offroad Association's Battle Born Trail System routes, and we didn't know about it at the time, so it's on the list for next time. The way out toward Mina also comes close to their Battle Born Trail.

Find all three on onX, posted by Bonny Blue of Nevada: Old Cabin Squeeze, Gabbs Range Traverse and Washout Canyon Spur.

Loop off the highway
Start / end
Both ends on SR 839, about 15 mi south of US 50

Old Cabin Squeeze is a narrow 3.9-mile two-track on the east side of State Route (SR) 839, roughly 15 miles south of U.S. Route 50 (US 50). It leaves SR 839 and rejoins the highway farther south, so it makes an easy add-on to a drive down 839. Rocks pinch the track in places, and full-width 4x4s need a ground guide to keep their sidewalls off them. Near the middle, an old weathered cabin and a fiberglass outhouse sit beside the trail. The outhouse isn't serviceable. Verizon had cell service along the trail in October 2026, and no other vehicles were out that day. Big turnouts at both ends make easy staging areas. The trail crosses Bureau of Land Management (BLM) land, and no permits are required.

Gabbs Range Traverse cuts east for 12.4 miles through the Gabbs Valley Range, from State Route (SR) 361 to Finger Rock Road. The trail starts on SR 361 about 7 miles north of Rabbit Springs Road. Sharp rocks cover much of the track, and short, steep climbs and deep, narrow washes make it technical. About 7 miles in, Washout Canyon Spur branches south, climbs, then drops into a canyon. As of October 2026, a washout near the bottom of the canyon blocks full-size 4x4s, so the spur is out and back with no way through to the south. Verizon had no cell service on this trail in October 2026, and no other vehicles were out that day. Big turnouts at both ends make easy staging areas. The trail crosses Bureau of Land Management (BLM) land, and no permits are required.

Finger Rock Road offers three ways out. North leads back toward SR 361 and Gabbs. South, a few miles on, Finger Rock Road meets Dump Run Road. West on Dump Run Road follows maintained dirt to U.S. Route 95 (US 95) near Mina, 18 miles from the end of the trail. East on Dump Run Road leads to Gabbs Pole Line Road, the longest way out.

3.3 mi each way

Intermediate 5

Start / end
Leaves Gabbs Range Traverse about 7 mi in; ends at a washout in the canyon

Washout Canyon Spur leaves Gabbs Range Traverse about 7 miles in and runs 3.3 miles south, climbing first and then dropping into a canyon. Deep, narrow washes cross the track, and some come close to the limit for a full-size 4x4. A narrower or more off-camber wash would stop one. As of October 2026, a washout at the end of the track blocks full-size 4x4s from continuing through the canyon. Turning around there takes a multi-point turn, then the spur returns to the Traverse the same way. There's no Verizon cell service in the canyon. The spur crosses Bureau of Land Management (BLM) land, and no permits are required.
